STAGE 1

UrbanSound8K Dataset & Acoustic Taxonomy

The UrbanSound8K dataset comprises 8,732 labeled audio excerpts (≤ 4.0 seconds) partitioned across 10 balanced folds. The 10 categories represent urban environmental noise scenarios:

❄️
Air Conditioner
Stationary mechanical hum; continuous low-to-mid frequency resonance.
📯
Car Horn
Harmonic alert pulse; dominant spectral peaks between 2–4 kHz.
👧
Children Playing
Dynamic vocal formants; highly irregular polyphonic signals.
🐕
Dog Bark
Impulsive biological acoustic transients; rapid attack and decay.
🔧
Drilling
High-frequency mechanical friction noise; strong harmonic sidebands.
🚗
Engine Idling
Low-frequency fundamental combustion pulses (sub-250 Hz dominance).
💥
Gun Shot
Instantaneous high-energy acoustic blast wave; steep impulse onset.
🔨
Jackhammer
Periodic high-impact percussive transients (10–25 Hz cycle).
🚨
Siren
Frequency-modulated periodic sweep with strong acoustic harmonics.
🎵
Street Music
Complex polyphonic spectral mixture of instrumental melodies.
STAGE 2

Acoustic Preprocessing & Feature Extraction

Acoustic signals are loaded via librosa at a standardized sampling rate of fs = 22,050 Hz:

Parameter / Feature Configuration Value Physical / Acoustic Justification
Sampling Rate (fs) 22,050 Hz Satisfies Nyquist criterion up to 11.025 kHz, covering human hearing and critical urban acoustics.
STFT Window & Hop Size Nfft = 2048, Hop = 512 Provides 23 ms time resolution and 10.7 Hz frequency resolution.
Mel Filterbank M = 128 filters (0 to 11.025 kHz) Non-linear frequency scaling approximating the human cochlear critical band perceptual scale.
MFCC Dimension 40 Coefficients DCT-II decorrelation of log-filterbank energies, capturing spectral envelope and formant contours.
STAGE 3

Additive Noise Robustness & SNR Simulation Protocol

To evaluate model reliability under harsh acoustic interference, calibrated additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) is injected dynamically according to specified Signal-to-Noise Ratios (SNR):

$$P_{\text{signal}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N y_i^2, \qquad P_{\text{noise}} = \frac{P_{\text{signal}}}{10^{\text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} / 10}}, \qquad y_{\text{noisy}} = y + \mathcal{N}(0, \sqrt{P_{\text{noise}}})$$

Testing across +20 dB, +10 dB, 0 dB, and −5 dB reveals the breakdown threshold for each acoustic modeling paradigm.
